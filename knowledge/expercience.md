During my engineering studies at ENIS, I have had the opportunity to gain both academic and practical experience in software engineering and artificial intelligence.

I completed two internships in an ERP company, where I worked on real-world software projects. These experiences allowed me to move beyond academic exercises and understand how software is actually developed in a professional environment.

During these internships, I worked on different parts of applications, including frontend development, backend development, databases, REST APIs, authentication, and application integration. I also worked on implementing new features, debugging existing functionalities, improving applications, and collaborating with developers using tools such as Git and GitHub.

What was particularly valuable for me was that I was not limited to one specific technology or one project. I had the opportunity to work on several different problems, which helped me become more adaptable and taught me how to understand an existing codebase, identify a problem, design a solution, implement it, and test it.

At the same time, I developed a strong interest in artificial intelligence. I started working extensively with Python, Machine Learning, Deep Learning, Computer Vision, and Natural Language Processing.

For example, I developed a computer-vision fitness coach using MediaPipe and OpenCV. The system detects human body landmarks, calculates joint angles, counts repetitions, evaluates movement quality using fuzzy logic, and provides real-time visual and voice feedback.

I also worked on intelligent document processing, where I combined traditional Tesseract OCR with the Donut Transformer to extract structured information from invoices, such as the invoice number, date, total amount, VAT, supplier, customer, and payment information.

Another important project was a photovoltaic project-management platform, where I combined full-stack development with machine learning. I worked with Angular, Node.js, Express, MongoDB, FastAPI, and XGBoost to build a platform capable of managing photovoltaic projects and predicting task durations based on factors such as complexity, priority, technician experience, weather, and season.

I also explored Big Data and distributed systems by building a Twitter analysis platform using Hadoop. I worked with a multi-node Hadoop cluster, HDFS, MapReduce, Selenium, Flask, and Angular. The system collects data, stores it in HDFS, processes it with MapReduce, and exposes the results through an API and dashboard.

More recently, I have been focusing heavily on LLMs and AI agents. I have been working with Transformers, LangChain, LangGraph, RAG, vector databases, tool calling, memory, and multi-agent architectures.

One of my main learning philosophies is that I like to build things from scratch. For example, I worked on an LLM-from-scratch project to understand tokenization, embeddings, attention, Transformer blocks, and text generation. I also implemented a WebSocket system from scratch to understand TCP communication, the WebSocket handshake, frames, masking, and protocol handling.

For me, building something from scratch is not about reinventing existing technologies. It is about understanding what happens underneath the abstraction. Once I understand the fundamentals, I can use frameworks and libraries much more effectively.

Currently, I am moving toward building complete AI systems, where machine-learning models are connected to real-time data, APIs, distributed infrastructure, IoT devices, and AI agents.

One example is HPIS — Human Performance Intelligence System, where I am working on an architecture combining wearable data, ECG and other physiological signals, Kafka, Cassandra, Redis, RabbitMQ, machine-learning models, and AI agents to provide personalized insights and recommendations.

Overall, I would describe my experience as a combination of software engineering, AI engineering, and systems engineering. My goal is not only to train models, but to understand and build the complete system around them — from data collection and processing, to models, APIs, distributed infrastructure, and the final application
